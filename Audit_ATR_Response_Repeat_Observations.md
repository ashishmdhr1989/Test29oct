# Audit ATR Responses — Repeat Observations

## Committee's General Observation

The Committee, taking note of the repeat issue, was not convinced and suggested that repeat issues should be taken seriously and addressed to prevent recurrence. A mechanism is required, including sharing the concept with all, informing concerned staff of the consequences, and sharing the plan with IAD as an update.

## 1. Absence of Exhaust Fans in Branch Vault Rooms

**Response**

As per the recent update, 17 branches have reported non-placement of exhaust fans.

Where installation of an exhaust fan is not possible due to infrastructure limitations, a table fan shall be operated during cash-in and cash-out times so that trapped moisture is released.

Further, the daily check of the exhaust fan shall be incorporated into the daily checklist circulated by the Security Consultant, so that the concerned OI confirms that the exhaust fan in the vault and locker room is working.

## 2. Security Lapses at Salyan and Pancheshwar Branches

**Committee direction**

The Committee noted security lapses at Salyan and Pancheswar branches and directed the Department to ensure installation of an iron gate and maintain safety standards as per Bank policy. The Department was instructed to obtain clarification from the Security Consultant regarding failure to maintain the required arrangements at all branches.

**Response**

In line with the Committee’s directive and in consultation with the Security Consultant, the Bank has undertaken a phased implementation plan to strengthen physical security arrangements across all branches. At the commencement of the project, only 74 out of 265 branches had heavy metal doors installed. Branch-wise risk assessment and prioritization were carried out in coordination with the Security Consultant.

- **Phase 1:** Metal doors were installed at lockers and vaults of 17 CCMC branches.
- **Phase 2:** Installations were completed at additional high-priority branches with locker facilities, as recommended by the Security Consultant.
- **Phase 3:** The remaining branches were covered, including rural locations and branches within Kathmandu Valley.

Installation of metal doors at both Salyan Branch (26 August 2026) and Pancheshwor Branch (2 September 2026), specifically highlighted by the Committee, has been completed. Installation work at the remaining branches is also in progress, with only 11 branches (8 inside the Valley and 3 outside the Valley) pending completion. These are expected to be completed by the end of Kartik due to the festive period.

For branches where installation could not be completed due to building-related structural limitations, alternative solutions are being explored in some cases. The response notes the following constraints:

- **Bakumari and Sallaghari branches:** The vault entrance is aligned with a structural beam/pillar, preventing installation of a standard-sized metal door. Customization options are under review.
- **Koteshwor Branch:** The building is non-RCC, and the landlord has not permitted installation of the metal door.
- **Mangalbazar Branch:** The traditional Newari building structure, narrow staircase, and congested access passage make installation of a standard metal door infeasible.

With installations completed at Salyan and Pancheshwor and implementation continuing at the remaining locations, the Department states that it has substantially addressed the Committee’s observations and continues to monitor pending actions to ensure compliance with the Bank’s security standards.

## 3. Verification, Recording and Tagging of Assets

**Committee direction**

The Department was instructed to provide compliance confirmation that all assets had been verified, recorded and tagged.

**Response**

The Department confirms that a comprehensive fixed-asset verification and tagging exercise has been carried out across the branch network through the Half-Yearly Fixed Assets Verification process.

In the most recent verification conducted in Shrawan 2083, all 264 branches submitted confirmations regarding physical verification and asset-tagging status. Ten branches reported exceptions, and Admin is coordinating with them to resolve the discrepancies. Of these 10 branches, 2 have already reconciled the assets and 8 are still in process.

## 4. Delay in Finalizing the Vehicle Management Policy

**Committee direction**

The Committee noted that the Vehicle Management Policy had remained pending for a considerable period and instructed the Department to explain the reasons for the delay and complete it.

**Response**

The delay in finalizing the Vehicle Management Policy was primarily due to extensive review, stakeholder consultation, and alignment on key policy provisions. The draft policy underwent detailed discussions among the Human Resources, Finance, and Administration Departments, resulting in proposed amendments intended to ensure operational practicality, cost efficiency, and policy consistency.

The policy was subsequently deliberated at DCEO and CEO levels, where two critical matters required further consideration:

1. **Vehicle Upkeep Allowance:** Eligibility, coverage, and financial implications were considered; it was ultimately suggested that the allowance be dropped.
2. **Manager Vehicle Disposal Strategy:** It was suggested that the matter proceed as per Bank policy and be addressed during amendment of the Financial Administrative Bylaws.

Given the significance and long-term operational and financial impact of these issues, multiple rounds of review and consultation were undertaken for appropriate governance oversight.

Following detailed assessment and resolution of the outstanding issues, the Vehicle Management Policy has been finalized and forwarded for formal approval and signing by the Board. No further substantive issues remain pending from the Department’s side. The Department expects implementation upon obtaining Board approval, addressing the Committee’s concern regarding the prolonged pendency of the policy.

## 5. Issuance of Guarantees for Proprietary Work

**Committee direction**

The Committee viewed the issuance of a guarantee for proprietary work as a very serious lapse and instructed that it be corrected and strictly avoided in future.

**Response**

The issue was identified in relation to the Janakpur Pidarichowk Construction Project undertaken during the previous fiscal year, which has since been completed and handed over. Following escalation through the audit process, the Department immediately reviewed the practice and implemented corrective measures to prevent recurrence.

The Bank has discontinued acceptance of Performance Bank Guarantees (PBGs) and Advance Payment Guarantees (APGs) issued by Nabil Bank Ltd. for contracts and projects where Nabil Bank itself is the beneficiary. For recent construction and procurement projects, vendors have been explicitly instructed to obtain such guarantees from other licensed financial institutions.

To institutionalize this control, the Department will incorporate a standard provision in all RFP notices, bid documents, and contract-related communications stating that guarantees issued by financial institutions other than Nabil Bank Ltd. are acceptable for submission.

The Department considers the lapse rectified and the concern addressed and closed, with controls established to prevent similar instances in future.

## 6. Standard Technical and Financial Assessment Format

**Committee direction**

The Committee raised concern over the lack of a standard Technical and Financial Assessment format and instructed the Department to treat the matter with utmost seriousness and take appropriate corrective action when preparing and presenting vendors’ comparative analyses.

**Response**

The Department has been conducting technical and financial evaluations in accordance with the Financial Administrative Bylaws, which prescribe a 50% weightage for Technical Assessment and 50% for Financial Assessment during vendor selection.

The audit concern specifically relates to the absence of a standardized comparative assessment format for enlisted/pre-qualified vendors. For recurring procurements, such as stationery, routine goods, and construction-related works, vendors are enlisted through a comprehensive pre-qualification process conducted every two years. During enlistment, detailed technical evaluations are undertaken using criteria including:

- Major projects completed
- Quality of past work
- Document compliance
- Experience with at least 2 Class A banks, or any 3 Class A/B banks, during the last 3 years
- Years of operation
- Feedback from peer banks

Only technically qualified vendors are approved by the Procurement Committee to participate in subsequent procurement exercises during the enlistment period.

For procurements from the approved vendor pool, technical eligibility is considered to have already been established through enlistment, and vendor selection is based on financial evaluation among pre-qualified bidders. This practice resulted in limited documentation and presentation of comparative technical assessments during individual procurement exercises, forming the basis of the audit observation.

Following the audit recommendation and the Committee’s concern, the matter has been brought to the Finance Department for incorporation into the forthcoming amendment of the Financial Administrative Bylaws. The requirement for technical analysis in every bid process is being re-evaluated for practicability as part of the ongoing review of the Financial Bylaws 2080. It is being proposed that separate technical and financial analysis may not be practical for every bid process, particularly for enlisted bidders.

Corrective measures have been initiated to strengthen procurement evaluation and improve transparency, consistency, and documentation standards in future vendor assessments.
